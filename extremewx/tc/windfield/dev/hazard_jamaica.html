Jamaica Tropical Cyclone Hazard Model

Drive a storm track across Jamaica and see the hazard footprints it leaves. Peak wind comes from the Chavas/Tao wind profile; storm-total rainfall comes from the physics-based TCR model (Zhu et al. 2013; Lu et al. 2018), including frictional convergence, vortex stretching, orographic lifting over the Blue Mountains, and vertical wind shear. Built on the idealized track explorer.

Development page, not linked from the site and excluded from search. The rainfall field has a known, unresolved defect: the cross-track profile oscillates by roughly a factor of five where it should fall off smoothly either side of the track. Output must not be used for any purpose.
Prototype — schematic terrain. No digital elevation model was available, so the topography here is a smooth analytic surface matching Jamaica's outline and summit heights but not its real slopes (mean ~2° where real hillslopes exceed 25°). Orographic rainfall patterns are therefore qualitative. Drop an SRTM/GEBCO raster in this folder and run build_terrain.py to swap in real data. Surge, landslide and inland flood are not yet implemented — they need the real DEM to mean anything.
