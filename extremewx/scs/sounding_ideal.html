Severe Convective Storm Environmental Sounding Explorer

A severe-storm environment is, to a good approximation, just two layers: a well-mixed boundary layer with constant moist static energy sitting under a free troposphere with a constant lapse rate, separated by a capping inversion. Set the eight thermodynamic and six kinematic parameters and watch the skew-T, the static-energy profile, the hodograph and every derived index respond live. Every curve is generated in the browser from the model of Chavas and Dawson (2021, J. Atmos. Sci.), ported to JavaScript. When you like a sounding, download it as a CM1 input_sounding file. Email me if you spot a bug or want a feature.

The math
The whole thermodynamic profile falls out of two static energies. Dry static energy is D = cpT + gz and moist static energy adds the latent term, M = cpT + Lvr + gz In the boundary layer both are constant: D = Dsfc gives a dry-adiabatic temperature profile, and M = Msfc reduces to constant mixing ratio, i.e. well mixed. At z = HBL the dry static energy jumps by ΔD — the capping inversion — and then increases linearly at rate βFT, which is exactly a constant lapse rate: ΓFT = Γd − βFT/cp Free-tropospheric relative humidity is held at RHFT,0, pressure comes from integrating hydrostatic balance upward from Psfc, and wherever the temperature would fall below Ttpp it is clipped there, producing a dry isothermal stratosphere whose base is the tropopause Htpp. (Paper §2d, steps 1–8.)

The math
AE17's scaling, which is the reason the model is phrased this way at all: CAPE is set by how far the boundary-layer moist static energy exceeds the free-tropospheric dry static energy, CAPE ~ (MBL − DFT) ln(TLFC/TLNB) while CIN is the same expression applied to the dry static energy difference across the cap, CIN ~ (DBL − DFT) ln(Tp,sfc/TLFC) That is why ΔD controls CIN almost by itself, and why moving RHsfc — which changes MBL but not DBL — moves CAPE without doing much to the cap. (Paper Eqs. 3–4.)

The math
Two unidirectional shear layers. Below HBL, purely meridional shear at rate cBL: u = usfc,   v = vsfc + cBLz Above it, purely zonal shear that may vary linearly with height, up to Hstop: ∂u/∂z = cFT,1 + cFT,2(z − HBL) integrating to u = usfc + cFT,1Δz + ½cFT,2Δz², with the wind held constant above Hstop. Storm motion is Bunkers et al. (2000); SRH is not part of the CD21 model, it is read off the profile afterwards. (Paper §2d, Eqs. 20–25.)

About this model

This page implements the idealized severe convective storm environmental sounding of Chavas and Dawson (2021), which builds on the two-layer static energy framework of Agard and Emanuel (2017). The thermodynamic profile is fully specified by eight parameters (Psfc, Tsfc, RHsfc, HBL, ΔD, βFT or equivalently ΓFT, RHFT,0, Ttpp) and the kinematic profile by six (usfc, vsfc, cBL, cFT,1, cFT,2, Hstop). Everything else on this page — CAPE, CIN, LCL, LFC, EL, SRH — is a diagnostic read off the resulting profile, not an input.

What the model does not do

  • The capping inversion is a discontinuity, not a finite-depth layer, and the moisture transition across it is equally sharp. Real inversions have depth and a gentler moisture gradient.
  • Free-tropospheric relative humidity is constant with height. Real profiles are usually C-shaped, moist near the surface and near the tropopause with a mid-level minimum.
  • There is a single free-tropospheric lapse rate, so a true elevated mixed layer — which would also have constant mixing ratio — is not represented.
  • Boundary-layer shear has no height dependence, and there is no shear at all above Hstop.

The paper discusses each of these as natural extensions; Jiang, Dawson and Chavas (2025) relax several of them. A sounding from this model has significant CAPE, significant shear and low CIN, which is necessary but nowhere near sufficient for any particular storm outcome.

Parcel ascent, and CAPE here vs. CAPE elsewhere

Parcel ascent is selectable in the sidebar. Pseudoadiabatic removes condensate as fast as it forms, so the parcel carries no water load — the usual assumption, and what most sounding software reports. Reversible carries every drop along: the condensate adds heat capacity, which makes the parcel warmer than a pseudoadiabatic one, yet the water it is dragging along makes it less buoyant, so CAPE drops by typically 20–30%. That inversion is the reason the distinction is worth making (Emanuel 1994, §4.7). Dry suppresses condensation entirely and is not a real CAPE calculation — the gap between it and the other two is exactly the latent heating. The optional ice toggle blends the latent heat of fusion and the saturation vapour pressure over ice across 0 to −40 °C, following Peters et al. (2022), which adds a few tens of J/kg. Both moist modes use one generalized lapse rate that differs only in the total-water term, so the comparison between them is clean. Beyond that, a different saturation vapour pressure formula will still shift CAPE by order 100 J/kg, so treat these numbers as internally consistent rather than as a match to any particular sounding package.
