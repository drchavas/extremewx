U.S. Extreme Weather Trend Map Explorer

County-level hazard days for hail, tornadoes and thunderstorm winds across the United States. A hazard day is any calendar day on which a county recorded at least one report meeting the chosen size or intensity threshold. Choose a hazard, a threshold, a season and a period, then map the long-term average, the linear trend, the change between halves of the period, or a single year. Pan and zoom anywhere; click a county for its full time series, seasonal cycle and year-by-month history. Source: NOAA/NCEI Storm Events Database. Email me if you see a bug or want a feature added!

Read trends with care. These are reports, not observations from a fixed network. Storm-report counts rose steeply from the 1950s into the 1990s as population, spotter networks, radar and NWS practice changed, so long-period trends partly measure how reporting evolved. Trends since ~2000 are far more meaningful, and higher thresholds (large hail, EF2+ tornadoes) are much less sensitive to reporting changes than "any report."
