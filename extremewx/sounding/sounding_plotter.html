Sounding Plotter

A skew-T log-p plotter for any sounding you like. Start from a preset, paste a table from the University of Wyoming archive, a spreadsheet or a CM1 input_sounding, edit any level in the table, or drag the temperature and dewpoint curves directly on the plot. CAPE, CIN, the parcel curves, the hodograph, the height-coordinate profiles of temperature, moisture, potential temperature and static energy, and every index update as you go. Everything runs in the browser — nothing is uploaded. For the physics-based idealized severe-storm sounding, see the SCS sounding explorer. Email me if you spot a bug or want a feature.

The energy buckets
Dry static energy is potential plus sensible energy, D = g z + cpT and moist static energy adds the latent energy of water vapour, M = g z + cpT + Lvqv Because g, cp and Lv are all near-constant, each term is a separate "bucket" that simply adds — the rightmost panel. That is what potential temperature cannot do: it is a nonlinear combination of temperature and pressure, and θe folds in water vapour nonlinearly on top of that. In a hydrostatic atmosphere D and θ carry the same vertical information, as do M and θe — compare the two middle panels. A dry-adiabatic layer is a vertical line in D; a well-mixed moist layer is a vertical line in M.

Note the two conventions: CP23 Eq. 2 uses specific humidity qv (plotted here), while CD21 Eq. 1 uses mixing ratio r. They differ by roughly 2% of the latent term. z is measured from sea level, so the surface-elevation box shifts the PE bucket and with it the absolute D and M; vertical differences are untouched.

How these are computed
The pasted or edited levels are interpolated onto a 500-point log-pressure grid, heights come from integrating hydrostatic balance upward from the surface level (so all heights are AGL), and every parcel is lifted pseudoadiabatically with buoyancy from virtual temperature. The effective inflow layer is the contiguous set of levels whose parcels have CAPE ≥ 100 J/kg and CIN ≥ −250 J/kg (Thompson et al. 2007). Storm motion is Bunkers et al. (2000); SRH uses the right mover. DCAPE descends the minimum-θe parcel between 50 and 400 hPa above the surface, saturated, to the ground. SCP = (MUCAPE/1000)(ESRH/50)(EBWD/20) with EBWD capped at 20 m/s and zeroed below 10; STP is the fixed-layer form using SBCAPE, SBLCL, 0–1 km SRH and 0–6 km shear.

What this is

A general-purpose sounding viewer and editor. It makes no assumptions about where the sounding came from — observed, model, idealized or invented — and computes the standard thermodynamic and kinematic diagnostics from whatever levels you give it. Alongside the usual skew-T and hodograph it plots the sounding in height coordinates — temperature, specific humidity, relative humidity, potential temperature and static energy, including the decomposition of moist static energy into potential, sensible and latent components.

Pasting soundings

  • University of Wyoming (weather.uwyo.edu) — copy the text block including the PRES HGHT TEMP DWPT header row.
  • Spreadsheet or CSV — a header row naming the columns is recognised (pres/p, temp/t, dwpt/td, drct/dir, sknt/spd, or u/v; mixr or relh work in place of dewpoint).
  • CM1 input_sounding — detected automatically from its shape. The file has no pressure in it, so pressure is recovered by integrating hydrostatic balance up from the header surface pressure.
  • No header — columns are read positionally as p, T, Td, and if there are five or more, wind direction and speed.

Caveats worth knowing

  • Heights are AGL, computed hydrostatically from the lowest level given. If your sounding starts above the ground, everything is relative to that level.
  • Parcel ascent is selectable: pseudoadiabatic (condensate removed — the usual assumption), reversible (condensate carried along, which makes the parcel warmer but less buoyant, cutting CAPE by 20–30%), or dry (no condensation at all). An optional ice toggle blends the latent heat of fusion across 0 to −40 °C. Even so, a different saturation vapour pressure formula will shift CAPE by order 100 J/kg, so these numbers will not match SHARPpy or MetPy to the last joule.
  • Composite indices are blunt instruments built for operational triage on real soundings. Applied to a hand-drawn or heavily idealized profile they can be badly misleading.
  • Diagnostics are only as good as the vertical resolution you provide — a 10-level sounding will give a noticeably different CAPE from the same profile at 200 levels.
